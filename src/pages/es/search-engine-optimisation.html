Getting Your Website Found on Google

While there are several search engines, Google accounts for approximately 90% of all search activity. SEO is often perceived as opaque, but proven methods exist for improving rankings — though results vary considerably by industry.

Google is also continually evolving. The introduction of AI search has shifted the landscape: where informational content once drove significant organic traffic, AI now answers many user questions directly within the search results, reducing click-through to source sites. The implication is clear — transactional keywords should now be the strategic focus.

What Are Keywords?

Keywords are the terms people enter into Google. "Plumbers in Arlington TX" is an example of a transactional keyword: the searcher has a clear intent and a defined location. These are the queries that produce direct revenue.

Informational keywords (for example, "how to unblock a shower drain") have historically been valuable for content marketing but are increasingly absorbed by AI-powered answers. Educational content can still build trust and capture readers who later become customers, but transactional keywords should now drive the core SEO strategy.

How Google Evaluates Websites

A useful way to think about Google is as a trusted referrer that only recommends a business when:

  • The business is clearly capable of delivering the work and is positioned as an expert in the relevant area.
  • The business is reliable and presents itself professionally.
  • The business is well-regarded, especially by recognized voices within the industry.

The criteria below explain how Google translates these factors into rankings.

On-Page SEO

On-page SEO is the most direct lever. It governs the words on your pages and how clearly they signal your area of expertise. There are three primary elements to optimize:

  1. Page Title — The headline displayed on the search results page (SERP). Multi-page websites benefit from multiple page titles — for example, "Plumber in Arlington" and "Plumber in Fort Worth" — expanding your visibility across related searches.

  2. Page Description — The text shown beneath the title on the SERP. Aim for approximately 160 characters and reinforce the page title naturally, including the target keyword at least once.

  3. Page Content — The body content visitors actually read. The target keyword should appear in heading tags and naturally throughout the page. Avoid keyword stuffing — the text should read fluently.

Executing all three consistently signals your areas of expertise to Google and supports stronger rankings.

Technical SEO

Technical SEO communicates professionalism and helps Google interpret your website effectively. The single most important technical factor is website speed — one of the clearest indicators of the difference between a designer and a developer.

A well-developed website performs strongly on Google's own tools. Google PageSpeed Insights evaluates websites on four metrics, scored out of 100:

  • Performance — How quickly does the website load?
  • Accessibility — Is the website usable by people with disabilities?
  • Best Practices — Does the website apply current web standards?
  • SEO — Can Google easily read and interpret the content?

For competitive search rankings, the goal is 90 or higher on every metric. GTMetrix is a complementary tool, with an A grade as the target. Achieving these benchmarks satisfies both Google and your visitors.

Off-Page SEO

Off-page SEO can elevate rankings significantly, though it is the most challenging to execute. Earning backlinks from reputable, relevant sources signals trustworthiness to Google, but the relevance of those sources is critical — not all backlinks carry equal weight.

The principle is straightforward: if you were seeking a recommendation for a nail salon, you would trust the input of someone in the beauty industry over an unrelated trade. Google applies the same logic. Backlinks from beauty industry sites strengthen another beauty business; backlinks from related trades strengthen a contractor.

This dynamic builds your domain authority. Google does not publish authority scores directly, so third-party tools provide approximations. Tools such as Ahrefs rate domains out of 100. Highly competitive industries typically require an authority score of 50 or above; less competitive industries can rank successfully with scores as low as 10 to 15.

What Does SEO Cost?

Cost is largely a function of competitive intensity in your industry. Highly competitive sectors typically require a substantial monthly investment in backlink acquisition, media coverage, and content production.
