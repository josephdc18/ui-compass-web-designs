I built on WordPress for years. Then I stopped.

Three reasons.

1. Plugin debt.
Every plugin you install is code someone else wrote, updated on their schedule, broken on their timeline. The average small business WordPress site runs 25+ plugins. Each one is a maintenance bill and a potential security hole.

2. The PageSpeed ceiling.
Most WordPress sites I audit score below 80 on mobile. The hand-coded sites I build score 95+. The gap is structural, not optimization. You can compress the images, lazy-load the scripts, and cache every page, and you still cannot outrun the framework's overhead.

3. The lock-in.
When something breaks at 11pm, you need a developer who knows your exact theme and plugin combination. Most "WordPress experts" know the dashboard, not the underlying code. Real fixes route to specialists charging $150 an hour.
