Sell Your Products or Services Online

So you want to sell online. There are a lot of ways to do that now — you can sell straight from your own website, run an eCommerce platform like Shopify, list on Amazon, or even run a TikTok Shop. Plenty of options. The question is which one is actually right for you.

It's not one-size-fits-all. Your marketing budget, your business priorities, and how well-known you already are all change the math on what works.

Why Does Marketing Budget Matter?

Let's be straight: eCommerce is brutal. You're competing against marketplaces and the biggest players in every category. To go head-to-head with them on paid ads, you usually need a real marketing budget.

If you don't have what the big retailers have, we have to think smart about how the site is structured and find other ways to bring people in. There are a lot of levers for eCommerce traffic depending on your budget and audience:

  • Google Ads — sponsored placements at the top of the search results. The goal is to appear first for products people are actively looking to buy. Pricey, but it works when the buyer intent is there.
  • Meta Ads — Facebook and Instagram, best for impulse buys. Works best when you've got a clear offer or discount, and usually needs A/B variations to find what actually converts.
  • TikTok Ads — perfect for showing products in motion. Skincare, cleaning products, food and drink, gadgets — anything that benefits from "look what this does" beats a static photo.
  • Pinterest Ads — strong for home, fashion, beauty, and lifestyle brands. The ads sit inside inspiration boards and don't feel like ads.
  • SEO — the slow, compounding lever. Faster site, cleaner structure, better content, higher Google rankings for your category. Pays off over months, not days.
  • Influencer Marketing — paying or partnering with creators with engaged followings to put your product in front of them.
  • Connected TV / Streaming Ads — what TV ads turned into. If you have a high-volume product and the budget for it, getting in front of millions of viewers still moves units.
  • Organic Social Media — posting consistently on your own channels to stay top-of-mind, show some personality, and build a community of repeat customers.
  • Affiliate Marketing — paying others a commission per sale to promote your product. Low risk because you only pay when an order actually closes.
  • In-Person / Experience Marketing — low-cost, especially effective for food, drink, beauty, and home goods. A market stall, a pop-up, free samples. Turning passers-by into customers in the moment.

What's Important in an eCommerce Store?

Website Speed

We've all been ready to buy and then watched a checkout sit there loading. What do you do? Hit back, find someone else. That's exactly why speed is non-negotiable for an eCommerce site. Your customer expects fast, and fast is what we build.

When you're spending real money on digital ads, the cheapest way to increase your return on ad spend isn't more ad budget — it's a faster site. More of the people you paid for actually land, browse, and check out. We've taken sites from double-digit load times down to two or three seconds, and the conversion lift is exactly what you'd expect.

Mobile Responsiveness

54% of web traffic comes from mobile, so your store has to work cleanly on every size of screen. We design from the smallest phone in active use (the iPhone SE) up through ultrawide monitors. The site reads its own viewport width and adapts to it.

That's what "responsive" actually means: the site responds to a resize, a rotation, a zoom-in, without anything breaking.

Easy to Use

Customers should be able to find products, browse them, add to cart, and check out without thinking about it. The fewer steps and the less friction between landing and paying, the better. Customers won't wander your site looking for the buy button — make it obvious or lose the sale.

That's the heart of good UX: a store that's enjoyable to use, not just functional.

Strong Design

Your store has to engage the customer the moment they arrive, especially if they came from a paid ad. The design needs to line up with the rest of your branding so the experience feels coherent no matter how someone found you.

That's why we design every store around your existing brand identity, so everything ties together end-to-end.

Why Does Trust and Reputation Matter?

Repeat Customers Grow the Business

Most people have a product they'll only buy from one brand. Once you have those customers, generating the next sale gets a lot cheaper. Email and SMS campaigns to your existing list, loyalty discounts, retargeting ads on Meta — all targeted at people who already know and trust you.

If you already have a strong base of returning customers, your dependence on paid acquisition drops. You can lean more on organic channels and existing relationships.

Reputation Is Everything

Most users won't come back after a bad experience. Let that sink in. eCommerce is a one-strike game. Your brand has to stay in good standing or sales drop quietly without you knowing exactly why.

This connects to everything else on this page: speed, ease of use, marketing, support. If any one of those slips, the rest gets harder.

Trust Matters More Than Ever

A lot of shoppers won't put a credit card into a site they don't recognize. The site has to earn that trust visually and structurally before anyone hits "buy." Everything has to look modern and professional, with no broken links, no typos, no "did this site get hacked?" red flags.

Most shoppers default to Amazon because they know their card data is safe, the order will arrive, and a refund is one click away if it doesn't. Your store has to compete with that confidence: clean checkout, recognizable payment gateways, real reviews, and a layout that doesn't look like it was assembled in 2014.
