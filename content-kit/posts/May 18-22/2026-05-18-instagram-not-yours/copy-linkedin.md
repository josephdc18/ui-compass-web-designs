Your Instagram handle is not yours.

If you spent the last three years building an audience there, that audience is leased from Instagram. They decide who sees your posts. They decide whether the account stays up. You can be doing nothing wrong, get hacked, get a strike for a policy you did not know existed, or just hit an algorithm change that drops your reach 70 percent overnight.

None of that is hypothetical. It happens every week to businesses we work with.

A website is different. A website is the only thing online that you actually own.

The domain is registered to you. The email list is stored on infrastructure you control. The content is indexed by Google under your name. If you stop posting for six months, your reach does not go to zero. The site still ranks, still gets found, still does the work.

You can have both. Most businesses should. The mistake is thinking the rental is enough.

The customer who hears about you at a dinner party opens their phone the next morning and types your business name into Google. If the first result is a website with a real address, real hours, and recent reviews, they call. If the first result is a Facebook page that has not been updated in three months, they hesitate. About half of prospects in that scenario will not pursue you further.

The website is the second handshake. Social is the first introduction. They are not interchangeable.
