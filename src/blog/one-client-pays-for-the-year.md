A managed website costs $1,800 a year.

If the site brings you one new client you would not have closed otherwise, you are above water. If it brings you two, you are profitable. Three and the website is the best-performing sales channel in the business.

Most service businesses clear the one-client bar in the first quarter.

Here is the math, with every assumption stated out loud.

## The cost

$150 a month, twelve months, $1,800 a year. No upfront. No surprise invoices. The monthly covers the build, the hosting, the maintenance, every edit you want to make for as long as you want to make it, and a full redesign every three years.

For frame of reference, $1,800 a year is:
- About 60 percent of the average small-business phone bill in DFW.
- One tradeshow booth, before staffing and materials.
- 36 boosted Facebook posts at $50 each.
- One-tenth of a part-time inside sales rep.

It is the cheapest sales channel a small business will buy this year.

## The bar

The website has to bring you, in twelve months, more than $1,800 of revenue you would not have had otherwise.

How big "one client" needs to be depends on your average customer value. Three quick examples:

- **Wedding photographer.** Average booking $3,500. One booking from the site, $1,700 in the green.
- **Plumber.** Average service call $400, average annual value per customer $1,200 (3 calls a year). One repeat customer, $600 to the negative on year one, $1,200 positive by year two.
- **Roofer.** Average install $12,000. One roof, $10,200 in the green. Site pays for itself in week one of year one.

The pattern is consistent. Higher ticket means faster breakeven. Lower ticket means you need a few more clients but the volume is usually there.

## The timing

We measure this on every build. The curve looks like this.

**Week 1.** Domain registered, hosting live, site indexed by Google.

**Week 2–4.** First rankings appear, usually for low-competition local searches ("[your service] [your suburb]"). Real but quiet.

**Day 30–60.** First-page rankings for the main service-plus-city searches. Direct traffic from people Googling your business name. Occasional contact form submissions.

**Day 60–90.** The first measurable lead arrives in the inbox. A real person who Googled you, found you, read for two minutes, and filled out the form. This is the lead that proves the math.

**Day 90 onward.** Compounding. Each piece of content keeps ranking. Each ranking keeps producing impressions. The site does not get more expensive as it works harder.

If you do not see the first lead by day 90, something is wrong and we fix it on the monthly. The site does not need to be replaced. The monthly model means we keep iterating until the math is true.

## The compounding part

This is where the website beats every other sales channel.

An ad costs the same on day 365 as it costs on day 1. Same dollar in, same probability of conversion. The faucet does not get cheaper.

A website is the opposite. The same homepage keeps ranking. The same blog post keeps producing search traffic. The same testimonial keeps closing the same skeptical visitor. Year two costs the same $1,800 as year one. The leads are 2x or 3x.

By year three, the math is not "did it pay for itself." The math is "what is the marginal cost of the lead." For most small businesses we work with, the marginal cost of a lead from the site in year three is under five dollars. There is no other sales channel that gets cheaper as it ages.

## What you have to do

Almost nothing.

You write us when you want a page changed, a photo swapped, a service added. We do the work. You forward us reviews and we put them on the site. You take a phone call from a lead. The site does the rest on its own.

The mistake owners make is assuming a website needs constant attention. <a href="/blog/managing-a-website/">It does not.</a> The right setup is silent. You notice it when leads come in.

## The honest part

The site will not bring you clients if your offer is unclear, your reviews are zero, or your service is not in demand in your area. The site amplifies what is there. It does not invent.

If you are unsure whether the math will work for you, reply with two numbers: your average customer's annual value, and the number of new customers you closed last quarter. We will run the breakeven on your specific business. If the site cannot clear the year, we will say so.

Most of the time, it can. The reason most owners do not have a website is not the math. It is the belief that the math has to be more complicated than this.

It is not.
