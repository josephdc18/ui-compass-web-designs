Not every small business needs a website on day one.

Owners feel guilty about not having one and they should not. There are real signs that say *now* and real signs that say *wait*.

Here are the five signs the time is now.

1. Customers are asking for a URL.
Someone refers you to a friend, the friend asks "Do they have a site?", and the trail goes cold. If this has happened three times in the last quarter, the website would have already paid for itself.

2. You are paying for traffic that has nowhere to land.
Ads, boosted posts, referral partners, sponsorships. Each one drives attention. Without a website, that attention lands on a phone number, a DM inbox, or a Google profile. All three have a conversion penalty.

3. Your competitor has one and you do not.
Even a mediocre site beats no site at all. Google ranks pages it has indexed. Your competitor's bad site is indexed. Your nothing is not.

4. You can describe your service in one sentence.
A clear one-sentence offer is the prerequisite for a working site. "I help small business owners in Dallas build hand-coded websites for $150 a month." If you can write your equivalent, you are ready.

5. You have at least one piece of real credibility.
One real review with a real name. One real photo of a real customer. One real credential. You do not need many. The first is the hardest.

If three or more of those are true, you are ready. If fewer, the website is not your problem yet. Something else is.
