We are going to be golden: “KPop Demon Hunters”

  • 기자명 Kang Dongyeon
  • 입력 2025.09.15 06:00
  • 수정 2025.09.15 13:01
  • 댓글 0

“Oh, I’m done hidin’, now I’m shining like I’m born to be / Oh, our time, no fears, no lies / That’s who we’re born to be,” sing the three members of HUNTR/X in their hit track “Golden.”

 

These powerful lyrics have become the cry for fans of Netflix’s latest animated sensation, “KPop Demon Hunters,” a film where music holds the power to save the world.

 

Released on June 20 and produced by Sony Pictures Animation, the animated feature has boasted an astonishing 220.8 million viewing hours, which is an estimation of 132.4 million views worldwide. According to a post on the official Netflix Instagram account, “KPop Demon Hunters” has been recorded as the most-watched original animated film on Netflix.

 

The film blends high-octane fantasy action with the colorful energy of K-pop. It is about a women trio forming a K-pop group HUNTR/X, composed of Rumi, Mira, and Zoey, who lead a double life. They boast a big fandom as a superstar, and at night, they protect the world from demons.

 

At the origin of their legacy, three female demon hunters had created the Golden Honmoon, which is a magical barrier powered by their voices to isolate the demon king Gwi‑Ma and his evil forces, who feed on human souls. HUNTR/X is the modern hunters, carrying out the legacy, always trying to protect humanity with their music, and strengthening the barrier into the legendary Golden Honmoon, a permanent defense.

 

Their endeavors are interrupted by the emergence of a rival demon group, the Saja Boys, led by Jinu, a half‑human, half-demon conspirator who tries to weaken HUNTR/X’s barrier to revive Gwi‑Ma and the negative forces.

 

At the Idol Awards, when HUNTR/ X performed “Golden” to unify fans and strengthen the Honmoon, Rumi’s demon nature was revealed, leaving Mira and Zoey feeling betrayed. Later, Rumi accepts herself as the way she is, and the trio reconciles again with understanding through music.

 

Along with the plot, the movie has captivated viewers with its vivid South Korean cultural elements, from Namsan Seoul Tower and the Seoul subway to traditional hanok houses, ramyeon, hanyak or herbal medicine, and jjimjilbang, which are dubbed bathhouses in the film. Also, it presents South Korea's Bukchon neighborhood and traditional motifs, such as norigae or knot designs, and gat, which is a traditional headwear. This authentically showcases South Korea’s rich cultural heritage, allowing global audiences to experience its traditions and modern vibrancy woven seamlessly into a thrilling fantasy narrative.

 

The soundtrack, including “Golden” by HUNTR/X and “Soda pop” by Saja Boys, is catchy and has made waves on Billboard’s main singles chart, Hot 100. On Aug. 11, Billboard reported that “Golden” went up a spot for its first week at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 chart.

 

Rumi’s journey depicts the significance of facing her truths and genuine self, being half-demon and half-human, and overcoming the fear of rejection. Our past and differences are what make each of us unique. Once she recognize this, she could embrace herself more fully.

 

Other members of HUNTR/X, Mira, having been called the problem child, and Zoey, always confused about her identity, have the opportunity to accept themselves for who they are. Mira breaks through and proves her value to her family by achieving success and fame through her activities in HUNTR/ X, and is especially known for her creative choreography.

 

As for Zoey, her ethnicity is South Korean, but she grew up in California. It was unclear to her where she belonged, and she regarded her messy ideas being all over the place as an uncontrollable defection. Gradually, she realized that her ideas were embodiments of her talent.

 

Throughout the movie, the audience could feel a strong sense of bond and affection between K-pop idols and the fandom, as well as within the group itself. Since support of the fandom is the motivation for K-pop idols in the entertainment industry, HUNTR/X always puts their fans first and cares about them in every situation. At the same time, the trio only succeeds in defeating the demons by staying together, despite doubts, misunderstandings, and fear.

 

The fact that the unwavering voices of the trio and fans strengthen the Honmoon barrier illustrates how music can heal, protect, and unite people. The film portrays creativity and art as weapons against hate and negative energy, resonating with K-pop’s realworld ability to bring communities together across borders.
