1 in 4 contact forms on small business sites does not actually deliver to the owner's inbox.

The form submits. The user sees a thank-you message. The owner sees nothing.

This is the cheapest insurance a small business website can buy, and it's almost always broken in the same four ways.

1. Send-to address.
The form is wired to a noreply@ or to an old admin email nobody monitors. Submissions land in a black hole.

2. SPF and DKIM.
Without these DNS records, your form's outgoing mail looks like spam to Gmail and Outlook. The notification is delivered — to the spam folder.

3. No honeypot.
Bots fill the form thousands of times a week. Without a honeypot field or basic captcha, real submissions get lost in the flood.

4. No confirmation reply.
The user submits and gets a generic "Thanks!" The owner has no idea who they were. Lead lost to ambiguity.

The 60-second audit:
- Submit your own form from a fake email
- Watch your inbox AND your spam folder for 5 minutes
- Check the headers when it arrives

If nothing shows up — or if it lands in spam — you're losing leads silently. Has been happening for months on most sites I audit.
