Don't sell. Hand out free mockups.

Your job at the booth is collect business cards, not close deals. The free mockup is the hook — it lets people say yes to something risk-free, and gives you a real reason to follow up later when they're actually thinking about their website.

01

The game plan

  1. 1
    Hook them in 5 seconds.One-liner only. If they don't slow down, you didn't lose anything — you planted a seed.
  2. 2
    If they stop — go 20 seconds.Hit pain → proof → price → risk reversal. Don't go longer than that until they ask a question.
  3. 3
    Let them ask.If they ask questions, you've already won the next 2 minutes. Answer short. Don't lecture.
  4. 4
    Close with the free mockup."Give me a card and I'll send you a free mockup next week." That's the ask. Almost no one says no.
  5. 5
    Stack their card. Move on.Don't try to talk price unless they push it. The deal closes in the follow-up email, not the booth.
02

The pitches

Memorize
When they walk by — 5 seconds

The one-liner

"I build hand-coded websites for small businesses — the kind that load instantly and rank higher on Google than the WordPress sites everyone else is selling."
Memorize
When they stop — 20 seconds

The full pitch

Say this "Most small business websites are built on WordPress or Wix — they're slow, get hacked, and Google buries them in search. I hand-code mine from scratch, so they load in under a second and score perfect 100s on Google's own performance test. Most sites score in the 50s. And here's the part people don't believe — $150 a month, nothing upfront, and I'll build you a free mockup before you ever pay me a dime. If you don't love it, you walk away and you're out nothing."

Hits 4 levers: pain (slow / hacked / buried) → proof (Google's own score) → price (no big number to flinch at) → risk reversal (free mockup).

The close
When you sense interest — wrap it up

The ask (don't skip this)

Say this "If you give me a card, I'll build you a free mockup of what your site could look like and send it over next week. If you like it, we talk. If not — no hard feelings, you got a free design out of it."

Don't ask: "Are you interested?" — that's a yes/no escape hatch.
Do ask: for the card. You're not asking them to buy, you're offering a gift.

Frame it as Google's verdict — not your brag.

Don't open with "I get perfect scores." That sounds gimmicky. Open with what the score means.

Say this "Google grades every website 0 to 100 on how fast it loads and performs. They use that grade to decide who shows up first in search. Most small business sites score in the 40s or 50s — mine score 100. That's not a marketing number, that's Google's own tool."

If they want to know why Google cares (Core Web Vitals)

Don't say "Largest Contentful Paint" or "Cumulative Layout Shift." Translate it.

Say this "Google's basically watching three things on your site: how fast it shows up, how fast it reacts when someone taps a button, and whether stuff jumps around while it's loading. Those three things decide your search ranking. Page builders fail all three. Hand-coded sites pass all three."

The "why does it matter" follow-up

Say this "If your site takes more than 3 seconds to load, most people leave before they ever see it. So you can have the best business in town and a great-looking site — and still be losing customers because Google decided you weren't worth showing first."
04

Objection handlers

"Why is it so cheap?"
"I'm a stay-at-home dad. No office, no employees, no overhead. I pass that on. It also means you're not getting handed off to a junior — it's me, every time."
"What's the catch with the monthly?"
"No catch. Site, hosting, updates, and me on call for changes — all in. Most agencies charge $3,000 upfront and then ghost you. I'd rather earn it every month."
"Can I edit it myself like WordPress?"
"If editing your own content is the main thing you need, WordPress is honestly the right tool — and I'll tell you that. But if you mostly need a site that works and brings in customers, hand-coded wins on every other metric."
"How long does it take?"
"Mockup in about a week. Full site usually 2–4 weeks once we're going."
"Is the mockup really free? What's the upsell?"
"There isn't one. The mockup is the demo. If you don't sign on, I just took an L on a few hours of work — that's my problem, not yours."
"I already have a website."
"Awesome — let me run a free PageSpeed audit on it. Takes me 2 minutes. If it scores great, I'll tell you. If not, I'll show you exactly what's costing you customers."
"What if I want to cancel?"
"You can. No long contracts. The whole point of monthly is that I have to keep earning it."
"How is yours different from a Wix site?"
"Wix builds your site like a Frankenstein in real time — every visitor waits while their server stitches it together. Mine is pre-built, lightning fast, and lives on servers all over the world so it can't go down."
"I don't really need a website."
"That's fair. But if customers ever Google you and don't find anything, they assume you're closed. Even a one-page site fixes that. Take a card — when you're ready, the mockup is free."
05

The rules

Do

  • Lead with their pain, not your tech
  • Say "Google scored it 100" — not "I get perfect scores"
  • Frame the price as "$150/mo, nothing upfront"
  • Always end with "give me a card, I'll send a free mockup"
  • Keep answers under 2 sentences unless they ask for more
  • Hand the card with the free mockup offer printed on the back
  • Smile, slow down, breathe between sentences

Don't

  • Don't say "static vs dynamic" — they don't care
  • Don't say "Largest Contentful Paint" or any jargon
  • Don't trash WordPress — respect it where it fits
  • Don't ask "Are you interested?" — too easy to say no
  • Don't quote setup fees — your model is no upfront
  • Don't get into a 5-minute rabbit hole on speed — trust the mockup to do the selling
  • Don't apologize for the price — it's a deal, act like it
If you blank — say this

The panic line

When you forget the pitch, when someone throws you off, when you've talked to 30 people and your brain is mush — fall back to this. It works on its own.

"I build websites for small businesses — $150 a month, nothing upfront, and I'll build you a free mockup before you pay anything. Want me to send you one?"
