# Blog Ideas — Pre-Website Audience

Long-form blog post ideas aimed at small business owners who **don't yet have a website** but are starting to consider one. Persuasive / educational, not technical. Common objections to address: cost, time, "I just use Facebook", AI confusion, "I'm not big enough yet."

Move an item to `src/blog/<slug>.md` once drafted. Keep a brief note here when shipped so we don't re-pitch.

Each idea below: **Title hook · the angle · suggested card template.**

---

## 1. Renting on social media vs owning a website

**Hook:** "Your Instagram handle isn't yours. The algorithm decides who sees your posts. A website is the only thing you actually own online."

**Angle:** Platform-risk. One algorithm change, one suspension, and the audience you spent years building disappears overnight. A website is the deed; social media is the rental. Counter the "I already have a Facebook page" objection head-on.

**Template:** comparison (Rent vs Own)

---

## 2. 5 signs you're ready for a website

**Hook:** "Not every business needs a website on day one. Here are the five signals that say *now*."

**Angle:** Diagnostic. People feel guilty for not having one — this gives them permission to wait *or* commit. Signs like "customers keep asking for one," "your competitor has one," "you're losing leads to voicemail tag," "Google Maps sends people to a dead end."

**Template:** audit-checklist (5 checkboxes with verdicts)

---

## 3. What does a small business website actually cost?

**Hook:** "$500 to $50,000. Here's what each tier actually gets you."

**Angle:** Pricing transparency. Most owners have no anchor point. Walk through the real tiers — DIY ($0–$200/mo), Fiverr / overseas freelancer ($500 once), local agency ($5–15K), specialist ($150–500/mo). Show what you *actually* get at each, not just price.

**Template:** comparison (multi-tier) OR stat ("$150/mo" anchored)

---

## 4. One new client covers the year. The math.

**Hook:** "If your site brings you one new client in twelve months, it pays for itself. Most do it in three."

**Angle:** ROI math. Take their average customer lifetime value, compare to $150/mo × 12 = $1,800. Almost every service business clears one client from a website in the first quarter. Reframe "monthly subscription" as "cheapest sales channel you'll ever buy."

**Template:** stat ("1 client" or "$1,800")

---

## 5. What makes a small business website actually work

**Hook:** "A pretty website that nobody finds is just an expensive business card."

**Angle:** Four criteria. **Findable** (SEO). **Believable** (trust signals). **Fast** (load time). **Convertible** (one clear action). Without all four, the site is decoration. Each one is a separate failure mode.

**Template:** anatomy (4 callouts on a phone)

---

## 6. AI-built websites: where they help, where they fail

**Hook:** "Yes, ChatGPT can build you a website. No, it can't keep you out of court."

**Angle:** Honest take on AI builders (Wix AI, Squarespace AI, Bolt, Lovable). Great for first drafts, layouts, copy. Bad at accessibility compliance, local SEO nuance, image optimization, security, mobile responsiveness past the obvious. Show what AI gets you to (60% of the way) and what's missing in the last 40%.

**Template:** comparison (AI alone vs AI + human)

---

## 7. Drag-and-drop vs hiring someone: what you actually get

**Hook:** "Squarespace is $200/year. We're $1,800/year. Here's what the extra $1,600 buys you."

**Angle:** Be transparent about the trade-off. DIY = your time + a templated site you'll fight with. Hire = their time + a custom site + ongoing updates + no rabbit holes. Quantify the hourly cost of DIY ("if your time is worth $50/hr, 30 hours of Squarespace = $1,500").

**Template:** comparison

---

## 8. What "managing a website" really means

**Hook:** "Most owners think a website is a thing they have to babysit. The right one isn't."

**Angle:** Debunk the maintenance fear. The actual work: domain renewal (once a year, 5 minutes), content updates (you write, we publish), security/uptime (us, invisible). Total owner time per month: less than the time spent checking email for an hour. Compare to keeping the social accounts fed.

**Template:** audit-checklist (3 rows: your time, our time, total)

---

## 9. A website is an asset, not an expense

**Hook:** "On your tax return, it's a deductible. In your business, it's equity. The mailbox stops working when you stop paying. A website keeps showing up."

**Angle:** Reframe the accounting. Tax credit for web work, Section 179 depreciation, brand equity compounds over time. Counter the "monthly subscription = wasteful" framing. The site shows up at 3am whether you're working or not.

**Template:** manifesto OR stat (tax-credit angle)

---

## 10. "I'll get a website when I'm bigger" is backwards

**Hook:** "The website is what gets you bigger. Waiting until you're 'ready' is waiting for the symptom to fix the cause."

**Angle:** Flip the order. Customers Google a business before they call it. Without a site, you lose the half of prospects who only do business with someone they can vet online first. Growth doesn't precede the site; it follows it.

**Template:** manifesto
