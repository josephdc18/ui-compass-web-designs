Hand-coded sites load up to 10× faster than page-builder sites. And the gap is not configurable.

That's not a benchmark cherry-picked from one comparison. It's the structural difference between two models of how a webpage gets made.

Page builder: assemble on demand.
Every visit, the server hits a database, loads themes and plugins, runs JavaScript on the server, glues it all together, then sends it. The fastest a complete HTML payload arrives is around 800ms after the click.

Hand-coded: ready to serve.
The HTML already exists. The server hands it over. Time to first byte: 60ms. Same content, different physics.

The cost of the gap shows up in three places.

1. Conversion.
Roughly 7% drop per second of delay. A page builder site running at 4 seconds vs a hand-coded site at sub-1 means a steady 20% revenue gap that never closes through ad-spend optimization.

2. Mobile.
Half your traffic. Phones on cellular networks pay the time-to-first-byte tax three times over.

3. Lock-in.
The choice locks in the day you pick the platform. You can optimize a page builder, but you cannot beat the structural floor.
