Yes, ChatGPT can build you a website.

No, it cannot keep you out of court.

In the last eighteen months, AI website builders have gotten genuinely good at the first part of the job. Layouts that would have taken a designer a week now appear in fifteen minutes. Copy drafts arrive in seconds. Image generation cleared the uncanny valley for stock-photo use cases.

That is the first 60 percent of the work. It is real. Use it.

The other 40 percent is where the trouble lives. Six specific things AI does not handle yet, all invisible until they bite.

1. Accessibility. AI builders default to image alt text like "image" or skip it entirely. They pick beautiful color palettes that fail the 4.5:1 contrast minimum. They put form labels visually on screen but do not connect them programmatically. Each individual fail is small. Together they form a credible ADA lawsuit, and over 5,000 of those landed in 2025.

2. Local SEO. AI does not know that "plumber in Plano" is the search term you need to rank for. It writes generic copy about plumbing. It does not add schema markup. It does not check NAP consistency. The site looks fine. It just does not get found.

3. Real-world speed. AI builders preview on fast laptops with fiber. Your customers are on a four-year-old phone on cellular. The site that loaded in 1.2 seconds in the preview loads in 7 seconds for them.

4. Image optimization. AI accepts whatever image you upload. The 4MB photo stays 4MB and ships to every mobile visitor.

5. Form structure. The form looks right and breaks silently. Missing SPF/DKIM. No honeypot. No confirmation reply. One in four contact forms silently fails to deliver.

6. Security and patching. AI ships the site. It does not maintain it. Six months later there is a vulnerability. AI is not there to catch it.

The right way to use AI is hybrid. Let it draft. Let a human ship.
