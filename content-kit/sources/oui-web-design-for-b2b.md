# Web Design for B2B

## Key themes (paraphrased)
- B2B purchases involve 6 to 10 decision-makers (newer research: 5 to 16 stakeholders across 4+ functional areas). Each role needs a different content path.
- Sales cycles span weeks to months. The site must support repeat visits and multiple stakeholders, not single-session conversion.
- B2B credibility is built on named, specific proof. "Sarah Chen, VP of Operations, Brightwell Logistics" beats anonymous quotes. "Reduced order processing time by 40%" beats "great improvements."
- B2B forms should be longer, not shorter. Capturing company size, timeline, budget, and service interest yields fewer but more qualified leads. The opposite of consumer best practice.
- Service pages should be problem-first, not solution-first. Lead with the buyer's pain to demonstrate understanding.
- Navigation should organize by buyer journey and industry vertical, not internal departments.

## Quotable claims / stats (verify before reuse)
- 6 to 10 decision-makers per typical B2B purchase.
- 5 to 16 stakeholders across 4 or more functional areas (newer research).

## Possible UI Compass angles
- Consumer best practice says shorten the form. B2B is the opposite. Longer forms yield fewer but more qualified leads when 6-to-10 stakeholders are evaluating you. → BACKLOG section: Conversion and UX, template: stat-hero
- Anonymous testimonials read as fake to a B2B buyer. Named role plus company plus a specific number is the credibility unit that actually moves a purchase committee. → BACKLOG section: Conversion and UX, template: reasons-list
- B2B service pages should open with the buyer's problem, not your solution. The problem-first structure that gets the prospect to keep reading. → BACKLOG section: Conversion and UX, template: process-steps
