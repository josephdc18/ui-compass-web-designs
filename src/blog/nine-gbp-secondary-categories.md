Google Business Profile lets you pick up to nine secondary categories alongside your primary. Most small business owners pick one (or zero) and leave eight ranking slots on the table.

This is one of the highest-leverage local SEO fixes available. Cost: ten minutes in your GBP dashboard. Effect: a measurable lift in how many adjacent queries your listing surfaces for, without diluting the primary category you already rank for.

This post is the way to fill the slots, plus three other underused GBP features that compound the same effect.

## Why the categories are the heaviest lever

Google's local algorithm decides which businesses to show for a given query in two phases. First, it builds a pool of "potentially relevant" listings — businesses whose categories overlap with the query intent. Then it ranks that pool by distance, reviews, link signal, NAP consistency, and a dozen other factors.

The category step is the gate. If your category does not match the query, you do not enter the pool. The ranking factors that follow only matter for businesses that already made the cut.

A typical small business has one accurate primary category and could honestly claim three to seven secondary categories. Most owners fill only the primary. That means their listing is being considered for one bucket of queries when it could be considered for eight.

## Picking the primary

The single category that matters most. Pick the most specific option that genuinely describes your core business.

### Specific beats general

"Web Design Agency" beats "Marketing Agency" because the visitor searching for "web design" is closer to ready to buy than the visitor searching for "marketing." The more specific category puts you in a smaller, hotter pool. You rank higher in a tighter market.

The trade is reach. A less-specific category surfaces in more queries. But "more queries" includes a lot of low-intent searches you would not convert anyway. The right primary is the one that matches the buyer at the moment they are typing the search.

### Categories unlock features

Beyond ranking, your primary category determines which features GBP enables on your listing. A "Restaurant" primary unlocks the menu field. A "Plumber" primary unlocks the service-area selector. A "Web Design Agency" primary unlocks the services and portfolio sections. Picking the right primary unlocks the right tools.

## Filling the eight secondary slots

The secondaries are where you reach the adjacent queries.

### Rule 1: Genuinely adjacent only

Do not pad. A sushi spot should not list "Pizza Restaurant" as a secondary, even if the algorithm would technically allow it. Irrelevant categories dilute the overall profile and sometimes trigger Google quality reviews. The bar is "would a real customer expect this category to describe us?" If the answer is yes, list it. If no, skip it.

### Rule 2: Cover the buyer's search vocabulary

The categories should map to the words your customers actually type. A bakery that does wedding work and birthday work might list:

- Primary: Bakery
- Secondary 1: Wedding Cake Shop
- Secondary 2: Birthday Cake Shop
- Secondary 3: Custom Cake Shop
- Secondary 4: Pastry Shop

Each secondary catches a slightly different search intent. Together they net the queries the primary alone would miss.

### Rule 3: Watch the quarterly list updates

Google updates available categories about once a quarter, without announcement. New categories appear, old ones merge. Re-check your selections every quarter and switch if a more-specific option has been added. The same cadence pairs with your [contact form audit](/blog/the-contact-form-audit/) and your [NAP audit](/blog/nap-consistency-four-phone-formats/).

## Three more GBP features most owners ignore

Categories are the biggest lever. Three smaller ones compound the effect once the categories are done.

### The 750-character description

GBP gives you 750 characters for your business description. The average small business uses about 200, mostly filled with generic copy like "We are committed to providing excellent service." That is wasted ranking surface and wasted conversion opportunity.

A well-written description does three things in 750 characters:

- **Names the service area specifically.** "Serving Arlington, Mansfield, and Grand Prairie since 2019" beats "Serving the DFW area."
- **Lists the actual services with the actual words customers use.** "Slab leak repair, drain unclogging, water heater replacement" beats "Comprehensive plumbing solutions."
- **Ends with a specific value proposition.** "All work warrantied for one year. Same-day appointments available." beats "Customer satisfaction guaranteed."

The same [trust-signal rules](/blog/trust-signals-that-move-the-needle/) apply — specific, verifiable, current. A vague description costs you the same way a vague homepage costs you.

### The Q&A pre-load

GBP has a Q&A feature most owners do not realize is open to them. You can post your own questions as the business owner, then answer them. The questions and answers appear on your listing in the same way customer-submitted Q&As do, and they are indexed by Google.

The right pre-load: five to ten questions you know customers ask you on the phone. "How long does a repair usually take?" "Do you offer financing?" "What areas do you serve?" Write the question. Submit it. Answer it from the business account. Then upvote your own answer (yes, this is allowed) so it appears at the top.

The pattern is the same as [FAQ schema on your service pages](/blog/faq-schema-3x-screen-space/) — surface common questions in the language customers use, and the engine matches them to relevant queries.

### The weekly Posts cadence

<span class="tooltip-term" data-tooltip="A Google Business Profile feature that lets you publish short updates (events, offers, news) directly on your listing. Posts expire after 6 months.">GBP Posts</span> expire after six months. If you posted once at launch and never again, your listing has been silent for half a year — and the algorithm reads silence as inactivity.

The cadence to aim for is one Post per week. The Posts do not have to be elaborate. Recent project photos, customer reviews you can quote, seasonal reminders, business updates. The signal Google reads is "this business is active." Even minimal weekly Posts beat a perfectly written quarterly Post.

## A 15-minute GBP fill-out

Block 15 minutes. Open your GBP dashboard. Walk through the four steps below.

### Step 1: Set the primary category (3 minutes)

Edit profile → Business information → Category. Type the most specific descriptor for your core service. If a more-specific option appears in the dropdown than what is currently set, switch.

### Step 2: Add the secondary categories (5 minutes)

In the same "Category" panel, click "Add another category." Add up to eight more, picking only categories that genuinely describe what you do. Aim for three to seven, not the full eight, unless the eight all apply.

### Step 3: Rewrite the description (5 minutes)

Edit profile → Description. Open the existing description. If it is under 500 characters and generic, replace it. Use the formula: service area + specific services + specific value proposition. Target 600–750 characters.

### Step 4: Schedule the next Post (2 minutes)

Click "Add update." Pick a recent project photo, a customer review, or a seasonal note. Add a one-paragraph caption. Hit publish. Set a calendar reminder for next week.

That is the foundation. Repeating the Post step weekly turns the foundation into compounding signal.

## What ranking change to expect

The same 4-to-8-week pattern as [NAP consistency](/blog/nap-consistency-four-phone-formats/). Google needs to re-crawl your listing and reprocess the new category selections. The first ranking changes usually appear within a month; the full benefit shows up over three to six months.

If your business is in a competitive market (more than 50 competitor listings nearby), the category fix alone might move you from position 12 to position 6. Combined with [review velocity](/blog/five-reviews-a-month-beats-thirty-in-a-week/) and clean NAP, the same business can reach top 3.
