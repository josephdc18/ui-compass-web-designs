Discovery Call Playbook

Listen.
Then recommend.

>>How the call with Luu should go — opener, discovery questions, the live recommendation, and the close. No quote on the call.

Prospect Luu — Bishop Arts Town Homes
Referred By Hong (realtor)
Call Length 30 min
01
Open ~30 sec

Warm, short,
then shut up.

"Hey Luu, it's Joseph. I appreciate you taking the time. A little bit about myself, I'm former military, now I am an electrical engineering student at the UTA and I started my web design company, UI Compass, to build websites for small businesses. Before I throw ideas at you, I'd love to understand what you're building. Mind walking me through it?" Then take notes. Don't pitch yet.
02
Discovery ~15–18 min

Ask in this order.

About them & the property
  • Tell me about the town homes — how many units, are they all at Bishop Arts or multiple locations?
  • Are these short-term stays, mid-term (30+ days, corporate/relo), or long-term leases?
  • Are you on Airbnb / VRBO today, or starting from scratch with direct booking?
About the goal
  • When you say booking page — are guests selecting dates and paying online, or is it more of a request-to-book / inquiry form?
  • What's the main reason you want a direct-booking site? Save on Airbnb fees? Build a brand? Corporate clients?
  • Do you have branding, photos, existing copy — or starting blank?
  • What does success look like 6 months after launch? The answer tells you brochure vs. revenue engine.
About scope, timeline & budget
  • Any deadline you're working toward — a launch date, a season you want to capture?
  • Have you talked to other developers or looked at platforms like Lodgify? Just trying to understand where your head's at.
  • Do you have a budget range in mind, or want me to send a couple of options? Seeds permission to quote later without surprise.
03
Recommendation Live · No final quote

Summarize,
then point a direction.

At the end of discovery, summarize what you heard and deliver a directional recommendation. Pick the branch that matches what he said.

Branch A — Simple
"Based on this, I think we can keep the booking side pretty straightforward — likely a custom booking page with an embedded booking or inquiry system. I'll price that separately from the main website so it's clear."
Branch B — Middle (most likely)
"Based on what you've told me, I don't think it makes sense to build the entire booking system from scratch right away. I'd recommend we build the website custom, then connect a professional booking engine so you get availability, payments, confirmations, and calendar syncing without reinventing all of that. I'll put together a quote with one or two options."
Branch C — Complex custom
"This is more than just a website booking form. There's reservation logic, payments, availability, policies, and possibly channel syncing involved. I can quote a custom version, but I'll also include a more cost-effective option using a proven booking platform."
Then pause

Watch his reaction. Lean in → go deeper. Polite nod → wrap and send the quote. Don't keep selling past the close.

Conditional demo offer — Branch C only
"If you want, I can hop on a 15-min video call and screen-share a fully custom booking system I built for another client — videography business, similar reservation logic. Gives you a sense of what 'custom' actually looks like end-to-end. Up to you — happy to just send the proposal too." Why conditional: showing a custom system to a simple-embed client anchors them to a $12K solution they don't need and makes you look like an upsell.
If he asks for a Lodgify example
"Sure, Lodgify has live demos on their site and there are hundreds of public examples — I'll drop 2–3 links in the proposal so you can compare." Don't pre-build a mockup. You're the developer, not the Lodgify rep.
04
Close ~30 sec

Clear next step,
specific date.

  • "I'll send over a proposal with 2–3 options by Thursday."
  • "Want to do a quick 15-min follow-up next week to walk through it?"
  • Get his email and best number.
  • Confirm decision-maker: "Is it just you, or are there other partners weighing in?"
Calendar Tip

Send the follow-up invite as 30 min, not an hour. Respects his time and forces focus.

05
Intake Form After yes — not before

Send the form after he signs.

Don't send the Website Content Strategy & SEO Intake Form before the call or in place of discovery.

  • It signals you're not engaged enough to talk first.
  • It's a content/SEO intake built for a brochure site — half of it doesn't map to a booking-driven STR site.
Right sequence
1 Call Discovery questions cover scope, budget, timeline, intent.
2 Proposal Quote based on what you heard. 2–3 options.
3 They Say Yes Then send the intake form to gather production assets.
Booking Operations addendum — send with the intake
  • Number of properties + addresses
  • Listed on which platforms today? (Airbnb / VRBO / Booking.com — with listing URLs for iCal sync)
  • Average nightly rate + seasonal variations
  • Min / max stay length
  • Check-in / check-out times
  • Cleaning fee, pet fee, extra guest fees
  • Deposit policy (full payment vs. deposit + balance)
  • Cancellation policy
  • Tax setup (Dallas hotel occupancy tax + state + local)
  • House rules / guest screening preferences
  • Existing photos per property (need pro shots or commissioning?)

You don't need this to quote. You need it to build. Keep it out of the first touch.

06
Things to Avoid

Don't do this.

  • Don't quote a final number on the call — you'll under-price under pressure.
  • Don't list features ("I can build X, Y, Z!") — let his needs pull the features out.
  • Don't dump the 5-embed comparison on him. He doesn't care about Lodgify vs. OwnerRez yet.
  • Don't apologize or hedge ("I'm not sure if I can…"). You can.
  • Don't offer the brother demo unless he's in the complex-custom lane.
TL;DR

Open warm.
Listen.
Recommend.

Open warm → listen → ask the discovery questions → give a directional recommendation using one of the three scripts → conditionally offer the demo → close with a clear next step and date. Send the intake form only after he says yes, with a booking-ops addendum.
