# Red Flags When Hiring a Web Designer

## Key themes (paraphrased)
- Eight red flags worth pricing in: no real portfolio, doesn't ask about your business, unclear ownership, nothing in writing, designer's own site is slow/broken, guaranteed #1 ranking promise, no post-launch plan, price-only decision.
- Ownership pattern: domain registered in the designer's name, hosting on the designer's account, no source code delivered. That triple lock-in is the most expensive footgun a small business owner buys.
- Portfolio diligence: right-click any portfolio site and view source. If it is Divi/Avada/Elementor markup priced as custom, the designer marked up a $60 theme as a $5,000 build.
- A designer's own site is the truest portfolio piece. If their homepage scores below 80 on PageSpeed, that is the standard you are hiring.
- "Guaranteed first page" is a fabrication. No vendor controls Google's ranking algorithm. Anyone selling that guarantee is either lying or planning to game it with tactics that earn a manual penalty.
- The contract minimum: scope, total cost, payment schedule, timeline, ownership of finished product and source files, cancellation terms, post-launch support details.
- Disappear-after-launch is its own failure mode. The car-without-oil-changes analogy.

## Quotable claims / stats (verify before reuse)
- Typical custom small business build range cited: $2,000 to $8,000.
- Standard deposit: 25% to 50% of total project cost.
- Typical timeline cited: 3 to 6 weeks for a standard small business build.
- Designer's own homepage PageSpeed benchmark: 80+.
- A designer should be able to show 5 to 10 actual built sites, not just templates.
