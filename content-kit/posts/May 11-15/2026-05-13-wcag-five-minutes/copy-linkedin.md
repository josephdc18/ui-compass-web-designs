96% of the top million websites fail a basic accessibility check.

Small businesses are the easiest targets for ADA web lawsuits. Settlements landed $5K to $25K in 2025, and the credible threat exists whether or not the business has a customer with a disability.

Here is the good news. You don't need an agency or a $400/mo overlay widget to find the most expensive problems. Three tests, five minutes flat, anyone can run on their own site.

1. Contrast — 1 minute.
Open WebAIM's contrast checker. Sample your body text against its background. Anything under 4.5:1 fails AA. Most small business sites fail their CTA buttons.

2. Keyboard — 2 minutes.
Tab through your whole homepage. Can you reach every link, every form field, every menu item without a mouse? Can you see where focus is at all times? If you lose the focus ring, screen-reader users lose it too.

3. Screen reader — 2 minutes.
Turn on VoiceOver (Mac) or Narrator (Windows). Listen to the homepage. If it announces "image image image" with no descriptions, your alt text is missing. If headings read out of order, your structure is broken.

That's the audit. If any of the three fails, you have a known accessibility liability — the same kind the demand-letter law firms screen for.
